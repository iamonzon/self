I was not very good until late 2022, when my constant learning started to pay off and got exposed to (well-designed) high-throughput architectures.

I was very lucky to start working side-by-side with a **principal engineer**. This experience exposed me to better practices,

- I learned about applied DSA:
  - sliding window for anomaly detection in metrics.
  - inverted index for quick matching in ad campaigns.
- How to measure before taking decisions: data driven decisions.
- I learned about observability, done right: cardinality, product and application metrics, profiling, alerts.
- How to build real scalable components: price calculations, napkin maths in cloud architectures.
- How to approach and design when a codebase is pretty complex: domain-driven-design in practice.
- How to design and make explicit trade-offs: RFCs and ADRs
- Being pragmatic on a code-base: use the paradigm (Imperative, OOP, FP) based on the need.
- Incident management: damage-control, troubleshooting, analysis, post-mortems.

Overall, I became more thoughtful and resourceful by getting exposed to what a real, good, software engineering is.
